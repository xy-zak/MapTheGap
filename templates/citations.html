00

What a line on the map means

A solid line A → B means A's reference list includes B, and both are in your library. Nothing else draws one.

Zotero doesn't record citations, so the map asks OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar for each item's references, then keeps the ones that point back into your library.

01

Identify each item in OpenAlex

Each item with a DOI is looked up:

GET https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1017/cbo9781316423936?mailto=you@example.com

This returns its OpenAlex ID, which other reference lists point at, plus its own references and citation count.

  • No DOI (most books, talks, blog posts): not looked up, unless an ID was confirmed by hand.
  • Split records. OpenAlex often splits a book across editions. Extra IDs can be listed so citations to any of them count.
  • arXiv IDs are read from the DOI, Archive ID, URL or Extra field.
  • Years come from Zotero, which you can correct, not from OpenAlex.

The contact address from setup gets faster service.

02

Fetch each reference list

OpenAlex's list is used when it isn't empty. Otherwise (often for preprints) the build asks Semantic Scholar, and keeps each reference's DOI and arXiv ID:

GET https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.48550/arxiv.2010.08168?fields=title,year,externalIds,references.externalIds

About one request every five seconds without an account, which is why the first build is slow.

03

Keep the references that point into your library

A → B is drawn when one of A's references matches B:

A line that points forward in time triggers a warning. It's usually a wrong date in Zotero.

04

Turn the lines into roles

rolerulein your library
foundationalcited by at least N sources here: the 80th percentile, never under 3
additionallinked to at least one other source, below that
contrariantagged role: contrarian by judgement; overrides the rest
satelliteno lines to anything in the library

Roles are recomputed every build and written back as role: tags. Contrarian can't be computed: a reference list says that one work cited another, not whether it agreed.

05

The dashed lines aren't citations

In clustering, a faint dashed line joins two sources that share at least half their subject:, question: and approach: tags (Jaccard ≥ 0.5) and have no citation between them. They share language, not influence. See tagging info.

06

Reading the map honestly

  • A missing line means missing index data, not missing influence.
  • Books, recordings and blog posts drift to satellite. Satellite never means unimportant.
  • Collections with no lines between them may really be separate.
  • Only successful lookups are saved, so failures are retried on the next build.
