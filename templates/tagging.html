What the tags are for

The map reads prefixed tags from Zotero in three ways:

  • Timeline bands. subject:, question: and approach: each split the timeline into swimlanes. A source sits in the band of its rarest tag.
  • Vocabulary links. Sources that share most of those tags get a faint dashed line, where no citation already joins them.
  • Cluster names. Each community is named after its most distinctive tag, evidence: included.

Untagged items still map, just in one band with no vocabulary links.

01

The four facets

Ask the four questions in order, and stop when you run out of answers.

facetthe question it answersper itemin your library
subject:What is it about?one or two
question:What problem does it address?two or three
approach:What tradition or technique do the authors work with?one or two
evidence:What kind of claim can it support?usually one
  • Aim for about six tags per item: under three is too thin, over nine is a list.
  • subject: cross-cutting is for items that span subjects, or are purely about method.
  • approach: is the tradition the authors work in, not everything they cite.
  • evidence: is the argument, not the format. A starting set: conceptual · case study · comparative cases · systematic review · formal model · benchmark / experiments · quasi-experimental · network analysis · dataset / product · popular / secondary.
02

Rules that keep the vocabulary usable

  • At most 15 values per facet. When one is full, merge or retire a value rather than stretching an existing one to fit.
  • Every value on at least two items.
  • Spell it exactly as it already exists. To the map, land use & planning and land use and planning are different tags.
  • Tag from the abstract or PDF, not the title alone.
style
caselowercase, except acronyms: approach: commons / CPR
lengtha noun phrase of two to four words, no verbs
joining&, not "and"; / for a pair or alternative
prefixfacet, colon, space, value: question: collective action
03

Two tags you don't hand out

  • role: is rewritten on every build. The exception is role: contrarian, a judgement that always wins, for items that argue against the foundational position (citation info).
  • MISSING marks a citation with no file attached (a link-only attachment counts). Not for blog posts or recordings.

05

Tagging by hand in Zotero

  1. Select an item, open Tags in the item pane and press +.
  2. Type the full tag, e.g. subject: forest. Pick Zotero's suggestion to keep the spelling exact.
  3. To tag many items, select them and drag them onto the tag in the tag selector, bottom left.
  4. Press update map once the strip counts the change.

Renaming a tag in the tag selector merges it everywhere, but bypasses the skill's record of retired terms.

06

Letting Claude tag for you

The zotero-tag skill in this repo applies these rules: it reads each abstract or PDF, proposes tags, writes them to zotero.org and flags its least certain call.

  1. Start Claude Code (claude) in the MapTheGap folder, where the skills live.
  2. Use a key with write access: setup step 02, or edit the key at zotero.org/settings/keys.
  3. Ask in plain words:

Tag the untagged items in my library. Propose the tags first and wait for me before writing anything.

the safe default

Tag the papers in my "Forest governance" collection.
